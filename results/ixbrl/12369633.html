The directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 March 2025 in respect of APEX Topco Limited (the “company”) and its subsidiaries, (collectively known as the “Group”). This report outlines the Group’s principal activities, reviews performance and key developments during the year, and provides insight into financial results, risks, and outlook.

Principal activities

The Group is a private equity–backed provider of multi-country payroll services and global mobility solutions for corporate clients. The Group’s principal activities include delivering integrated global and domestic payroll processing, expatriate tax services, and human resource (HR) support to organisations worldwide. Headquartered in Aberdeen, Scotland, and operating through regional hubs and partners around the globe, the Group serves over 500 companies across more than 140 countries. Its highly experienced team and worldwide footprint enable the Group to help clients navigate complex international payroll, tax, and compliance challenges while providing consistent, high-quality service. The Group’s mission is to support the payroll and mobility needs of growth-oriented businesses wherever they operate, leveraging technology and expertise to ensure accurate, compliant, and timely payroll operations.

 

Business Review

Overview

The year ended 31 March 2025 marked a step forward for the Group, building on the transitional and challenging prior period. Revenue grew year-on-year, reflecting resilient customer demand and the early benefits of strategic initiatives, while profitability was managed against the backdrop of continued investment.

 

A key focus during the year was strengthening the management of working capital, improving financial discipline and stability across the Group. At the same time, we continued to invest in our solutions to ensure customer delivery remained at the forefront of our strategy. This included targeted investment in both people and technology, supporting product development and enhancing operational efficiency.

 

Through these initiatives, the Group exits the year with a stronger foundation, a sharper focus on execution, and an enhanced capability to deliver sustainable, profitable growth in future periods.

 

Revenue and Performance

Group turnover for FY2025 increased to £57.4 million, up from £52.2 million in FY2024. This continued top-line expansion was driven by a full years contributions from the LIMES International BV acquisition, organic growth, new customer acquisitions and expanded engagements with existing clients, which broadened our geographic reach. Further initiatives related to customer services and Limes revenues were intensified in the latter part of the financial year and are continuing into FY2026. These efforts have supported both recurring revenue and sustained expansion.

 

A key driver of this sustained revenue growth is the Group’s unwavering focus on cultivating long-term, trusted relationships with clients. By consistently delivering high-quality, compliant payroll and mobility solutions, the Group has excellent customer retention, even amidst increasing competition in the global payroll sector. This commitment to customer success has translated into a steady increase in annual recurring revenue, underscoring the resilience and predictability of our revenue base.

 

The global payroll division performed well, with particularly strong demand in key markets. Meanwhile, the global mobility and tax advisory services saw increased activity as clients sought integrated support for their internationally mobile employees.

 

The directors believe that the strength and longevity of our customer relationships are fundamental to our sustained growth and market leadership. By prioritising client satisfaction and delivering consistent value, the Group continues to reinforce its position as a trusted partner in global payroll and mobility solutions.

Profitability

The Group’s operating loss for FY2025 was £3.9 million, compared to an operating loss of £6.5 million in FY2024.

 

The underlying trading performance remained robust. The directors monitor adjusted EBITDA* (as defined on page 3), FY2025 £13.1 million (FY2024 £13.0 million) as a key measure of underlying performance. There was a slight increase in adjusted EBITDA and the level of add-backs required to reach this figure fell significantly, from £7.3 million in FY2024 to £3.0 million in FY2025 indicating tighter control over adjusting items during the financial year.

 

The significant improvement in operating margin from -12.5% to -6.8% reflects both efficiency gains and a reduction in exceptional costs compared to the prior year. Whilst part of the improvement arose from one-off benefits, the Directors considers that the structural measures implemented, particularly the integration of operations, process automation and disciplined cost management, provide a sustainable platform for profitability going forward. Additionally, management has elected to release a portion of the bad debt provision, based on positive cash collection trends and strong quality of earnings.

 

Provision for doubtful debts

At year end, the Group completed a data‑driven review of trade receivables—combining ageing analysis, customer‑specific risk assessments, and observed cash collections during FY2025—to reassess expected credit losses in line with the provisioning policy introduced in FY2024 under FRS 102. This review supported a £0.8m reduction of the prior‑year £4.0m provision, reflecting improved recoveries and strengthened credit control processes, with a prudent residual provision of £3.2m maintained at 31 March 2025 (see Note 17). Provisioning levels and movement drivers are subject to formal quarterly balance sheet reviews by the Board and Audit Committee, which challenge key assumptions and coverage by ageing cohort to ensure the position remains appropriate and conservative over time.

 

Operational Improvement Initiatives

In FY2025 the Group built further momentum by extending the operational improvements initiated in the previous year. Efforts were concentrated on tightening working capital management and embedding stronger service delivery disciplines across the organisation. Investments were directed towards enhancing internal systems – such as payroll platform integrations and workflow automation – to increase scalability and ensure consistency in delivery across all markets. These actions contributed to higher levels of accuracy and timeliness in payroll processing, with client satisfaction remaining robust.

 

Alongside system improvements, greater emphasis was placed on developing people capability. Training programmes were broadened and employee engagement initiatives reinforced, resulting in improved productivity and a more resilient workforce. While the upfront cost of these programmes was reflected in the year’s results, they are already unlocking efficiencies and provide a clear path to margin improvement in the periods ahead.

 

Outlook

Looking ahead, the directors remain confident in the Group’s trajectory. The customer base continues to expand, supported by sustained demand for multi-jurisdictional payroll and mobility solutions. With the further integration of LIMES progressing and internal improvement programmes gaining traction, the Group is better positioned to deliver stronger financial performance in the years ahead.

 

Management’s priorities remain clear: disciplined working capital management, continued investment in technology and talent, and an uncompromising focus on service delivery. These priorities are designed to support scalable growth and improved profitability as efficiencies flow through from the initiatives undertaken in FY2025.

 

The breadth of the Group’s offering – spanning payroll, tax, mobility, and HR advisory – creates significant opportunity both within our existing client portfolio and through new client acquisition. Having navigated the challenges of the prior period and strengthened its platform in FY2025, the Group enters the new financial year with a stronger operational base, an enhanced client proposition, and a clear path to long-term value creation for shareholders.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The Group faces a range of risks and uncertainties inherent in its operations and the broader business environment. The Board regularly reviews these principal risks and has implemented policies and controls to mitigate them as part of our risk management framework. The following are the key risks and uncertainties identified for the Group:

 

  • Regulatory Compliance Risk: The Group operates in a highly regulated domain across numerous jurisdictions. Payroll and tax regulations change frequently and vary from country to country. Non-compliance or errors could lead to legal penalties, client dissatisfaction, and reputational damage. The Group mitigates this risk by maintaining rigorous compliance processes and investing in expert knowledge. We continuously monitor legislative changes in every jurisdiction we serve. Regular training, and quality control checks are in place to ensure adherence to all applicable laws and regulations.

 

  • Operational and Integration Risk: As the Group continues to scale and pursue growth opportunities, including through acquisitions, there is an inherent risk of operational disruption or delay in realising expected benefits. Key challenges include aligning IT systems, standardising processes, and managing change across different geographies and operating models. To mitigate these risks, the Group undertakes detailed planning for operational transitions, supported by experienced project teams and phased implementation strategies. Engagement with customers remains a priority throughout periods of change to ensure service continuity and maintain confidence. The Group remains focused on strengthening its operational infrastructure and controls to support sustainable growth and safeguard service quality.

 

  • Technology and Data Security Risk: The Group relies on sophisticated payroll software, cloud platforms, and IT infrastructure to deliver services. Any significant system downtime, cyber-attack, or data breach could severely impact our ability to serve clients and compromise sensitive personal data. Recognising this, the Group has made data security and system resilience a top priority. We employ robust cybersecurity measures, including data encryption, firewalls, access controls, and regular vulnerability assessments. Dedicated IT teams monitor system performance and have disaster recovery plans in place to ensure continuity of service. The Group is certified to high industry standards for information security and undergoes independent audits of its controls. Ongoing investments in technology upgrades and security enhancements help reduce the likelihood of disruptions and protect client data integrity.

  • Market and Competition Risk: The global payroll and HR solutions market is competitive and evolving. Competitors range from large multinational firms to local providers in various countries.   • Financial Risk (Currency and Credit): Credit risk arises from the possibility that clients may delay payments or, in rare cases, default on their obligations, affecting cash flow. To manage this, the Group conducts stringent client onboarding checks, maintains diversified client exposure, and employs active credit control measures.

 

During the prior year, the Group undertook a comprehensive review of its credit risk assessment procedures. As a result, the Group adopted a more conservative approach to estimating bad debt provisions, aligning with best practices under FRS 102. This change reflects a heightened focus on credit risk management and a commitment to prudent financial reporting.

 

The revised provisioning policy introduced in FY2024 had resulted in a one-off increase in bad debt expense, which reduced operating profit in that year. In FY2025, the impact of this change has normalised, and following a recurring review of trends and cash collections, management exercised judgment to release a portion of the provision over the course of the year. This reflects both the stabilisation of the credit risk environment and the benefits of strengthened receivables management.

 

Improved aged debt recovery during the year has further reduced outstanding receivables risk, supporting stronger working capital discipline. The Group also continues to maintain sufficient cash reserves and access to committed funding facilities, providing resilience against short-term fluctuations and reinforcing its ability to manage financial risks effectively.

 

The directors are satisfied that, with the aforementioned risk mitigations and the ongoing monitoring in place, the Group’s principal risks are being managed appropriately. There remains uncertainty in the external environment (including regulatory changes and economic conditions), but the Board is confident that the Group’s business model and prudent risk management approach provide a strong degree of resilience against these challenges.

Throughout the year ended 31 March 2025, the Board took into account the needs and priorities of these stakeholders in its decision-making processes. Below we outline how the directors have fulfilled their Section 172(1) responsibilities:

 

  • Employees: Our people are central to the business, and the directors have ensured that employee interests were considered in all major decisions. The Group held regular all-hands updates and management forums to explain the rationale for changes, address employee concerns, and gather feedback. The safety and welfare of employees remained a top priority, and the Board monitored staff engagement through surveys, employee net promoter, and direct interactions. By actively supporting and listening to our employees, the directors aim to foster a positive workplace culture that drives the Group’s long-term success.
