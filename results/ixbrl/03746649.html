Principal risks and uncertainties

The directors regularly conduct business risk assessments and have identified the following principal risks and uncertainties affecting the business and the key risks are as follows:

• Availability of skilled labour
A critical challenge for our business, as there is a widespread shortage across all trades. While we continue to invest in and develop our workforce through our award-winning apprenticeship programme, the rising costs of training, and increases to national insurance and national living wage make it increasingly difficult to sustain our current staffing levels and maintain a skilled workforce.

• Variability in construction programmes
A significant concern, as it can result in unpredictable demand for our services/ resources. Construction projects may be delayed due to a variety of reasons such as financing issues, planning permission hurdles, or broader market conditions. With periods of high demand followed by lower demand, it becomes increasingly difficult to manage workforce capacity effectively and maintain a consistent workflow. These cycles can lead to periods of uncertainty and affect our ability to plan for resource allocation and maintain profitability.

• Supply chain risks and manufacturers cost increases
Our business is heavily reliant on the timely and cost-effective supply of materials and equipment. Disruptions in the supply chain, such as delays in deliveries or shortages of key components, can significantly impact project timelines and overall costs. Changes in tariffs, or inflationary pressures, may result in higher procurement costs. These cost increases may not always be recoverable through price adjustments, especially in fixed-price contracts, and could therefore negatively affect our margins.

• Health and Safey issue/ event
Health and safety risks are inherent in the construction industry. Any incident, whether involving employees, contractors, or the public, can have severe consequences, including legal liabilities, reputational damage, and financial costs. Adherence to health and safety regulations is crucial to mitigating these risks. However, unforeseen events such as accidents, injuries can disrupt operations, lead to legal proceedings, and potentially result in the suspension of work on certain sites. Effective safety protocols and regular training are essential to reducing these risks, but the unpredictability of such events remains a significant concern. Our committment to mitigating health and safety risks is demonstrated further by our recent achievement of the ISO 45001 certification.

• External factors affecting the construction industry
External factors affecting the construction industry beyond our control, including but not limited to changes in legislation, inflation, interest rates, and government policy.
 

Future investment

We are committed to ongoing investment in the right technology, our people, and the wider community to ensure the long-term sustainability and growth of our company.

Our investment in IT systems continues, with the goal of enhancing both the customer experience and our internal operations. This aligns with our commitment to ISO 9001 standards and ensures we can provide an efficient and seamless service. Additionally, we have begun evaluating upgrades to our financial systems, aiming to provide more timely and accurate data that will support improved decision-making and operational efficiencies in the future.

We are also highly focused on reducing our environmental impact. Over the past year, we have made significant upgrades to our fleet, contributing to our environmental goals and reinforcing our commitment to ISO 14001 standards.

Our employees are central to our business success. We remain dedicated to providing continuous professional development (CPD) opportunities, enhancing engagement with our management team to identify key training and development needs. This approach helps improve productivity, unlocks the potential of individual employees, and fosters an inclusive culture across the business. We are also investing in joint training initiatives with leading manufacturers and clients, particularly in the area of renewable technologies. Our substantial efforts to upskill our workforce are aligned with the renewable energy sector's growth and the transition to more sustainable practices to support achieving net zero.

In the local community, we continue to support charitable causes, including St Peter’s Hospice, the Operation Christmas Hamper appeal and several local community football teams. This engagement reflects our ongoing commitment to giving back and supporting the communities in which we operate.

Raw materials and supply chain

The company values long-term, sustainable relationships with its raw material suppliers and is actively collaborating with key partners, including Priceworx, Wolseley, and City Plumbing, to pilot integrated systems. This collaboration aims to improve service delivery efficiencies, streamline processes, and enhance visibility. By aligning with our partners on sustainability goals and best practices, this initiative will enable more informed, timely purchasing decisions, ultimately driving improved outcomes for both the business and the environment.

In addition, to minimise material shortages and reduce the impact on productivity, we continue to strengthen our reputation with clients by ensuring reliable supply chain management and sustainable sourcing practices.


Cash flow and financial management

Due to the capital-intensive nature of our business, effective management of working capital is essential. Ensuring timely debt recovery is a process that demands significant time and resources. The board has consistently recognised the importance of prioritising this area to ensure prompt collection of debts, thereby maintaining a healthy cash conversion cycle. We are also continuing to invest in robust financial strategies to navigate potential financial challenges and mitigate the risks of any setbacks as we move into our new financial year.

Industry legislation

This is an exciting time for our company, presenting purposeful and forward-looking opportunities as the UK heating industry undergoes significant legislative changes driven by the government's decarbonisation objectives and commitment to enhancing energy efficiency, which continue to shape market demand. We remain well positioned to respond to legislative developments through our technical expertise, compliance standards, and continued investment in skills and technology.

Leases

Leases in which substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership are retained by the lessor are classified as operating leases. Payments made under operating leases are charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the period of the lease.

Leases are classified as finance leases whenever the terms of the lease transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee.

Assets held under finance leases are recognised at the lower of their fair value at inception of the lease and the present value of the minimum lease payments. These assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the useful life of the asset and the lease term. The corresponding liability to the lessor is included in the balance sheet as a finance lease obligation.

Lease payments are apportioned between finance costs in the profit and loss account and reduction of the lease obligation so as to achieve a constant periodic rate of interest on the remaining balance of the liability.

Defined contribution pension obligation

A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which fixed contributions are paid into a pension fund and the company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions even if the fund does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.

Contributions to defined contribution plans are recognised as employee benefit expense when they are due. If contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
